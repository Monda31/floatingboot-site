My kid drew every Italian stadium.

San Siro, the Maradona, the Olimpico — and fifteen more that never make anyone's souvenir shirt.

This one is personal, so I'll keep the claim simple and exact: my kid drew these. Not "inspired by a child's imagination," not a studio illustrator doing a cute-kid-art filter — actual drawings, on paper, of Italian football grounds. We printed them as they were. Eighteen grounds, one drawing each, one tee each, from the stadiums everyone already recognizes to the ones you only know if you're from there.

What this collection is not

It's not fine art, and we're not going to pretend otherwise — you won't find "detailed" or "precise" anywhere in how we describe it, because that's not what it is or what it's for. It's a kid's drawing: uneven color fill, a line that wanders exactly where a kid's hand wandered, letters that lean the way a kid's letters lean. That's the whole appeal. A polished illustration of a stadium is something a design studio could produce for anyone. This isn't that.

It's also not club merchandise, on purpose. Every ground here is a building tied to a city, not a team — we're not printing crests or colors or anything that belongs to a football club. This is about the place, the city, the Sunday you spent there, not who was playing that day or which side you were on.

The ones everyone knows

Some of these grounds don't need an introduction. Milan's San Siro, with its spiral access ramps and towering curves, is one of the largest and most recognizable stadiums in Europe. Rome's Olimpico sits inside the Foro Italico, ringed by marble statues from the 1930s. Naples' ground — officially the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona — carries a name that's now inseparable from the city itself. Big buildings, drawn the same size on the page as everything else on this list.

The ones nobody puts on a t-shirt

This is the part of the collection I actually care about most. Cittadella's ground holds a fraction of San Siro's crowd. Cremona's stadium sits in a city better known for violins than football. La Spezia's ground is tucked into a small Ligurian port town most tourists pass through on the way to somewhere else. None of them show up on a souvenir rack. All of them got exactly the same treatment as the famous three above — same size drawing, same tee, same $45. If you grew up going to one of these, this is probably the only place you'll ever find it on your own shirt.
